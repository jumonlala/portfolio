About Me

Welcome to my portfolio website!

My name is Jimin, a first-year student pursuing M.Sc. in Statistics at SFU.
I received a B.S. in Data Science at Washington University in St. Louis in 2026.

My research interest lie in developing statistical learning methods for high-dimensional, imbalanced data, with applications in survival and mortality analysis and fraud detection.
My interest in data science began with a broader curiosity about people and society. During my undergraduate studies, I explored questions across neuroscience, sociology, and biology—from how people think and behave to how biological systems shape human outcomes. Across these seemingly different disciplines, I noticed a common thread: data. Understanding behavior, explaining outcomes, and testing hypotheses all relied on collecting and analyzing data. I became fascinated by the idea that data could provide a way to investigate many of the questions I had about people and the world around them. That realization ultimately led me to pursue a Bachelor’s degree in Data Science at Washington University in St. Louis.

Data science introduced me to a wide range of approaches to working with data, from data visualization and management to statistical analysis. Through coursework and interdisciplinary projects, I learned to approach projects from end to end: understanding the problem, working with data, developing an analysis, and communicating the results. I had the opportunity to collaborate with researchers and teams across disciplines, including computational biology, financial statistics, and East Asian literature. These experiences taught me that effective data work extends beyond technical methods. It also requires understanding stakeholders' needs, communicating technical ideas to non-technical audiences, and keeping the analysis aligned with the goals of the project.

The more I studied data science, the more I became interested in statistics because it provides a framework for reasoning under uncertainty and understanding the limitations of models. I came to see a stronger foundation in statistics as an important part of becoming a more thoughtful and effective data scientist, which led me to pursue an M.Sc. in Statistics at Simon Fraser University (SFU).

At SFU, my interests have increasingly centered on statistical modeling for complex real-world data. Through exposure to actuarial science and survival analysis, I have developed a particular interest in questions surrounding survival, mortality, and longevity, including how demographic and societal factors are reflected in population-level outcomes. I am also interested in statistical problems involving rare and imbalanced events, such as fraud and natural disasters, where conventional modeling approaches can face additional challenges. I hope to continue developing these interests at SFU through coursework, interdisciplinary collaboration, and industry experience.
